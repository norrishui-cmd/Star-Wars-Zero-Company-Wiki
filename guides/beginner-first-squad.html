Star Wars Zero Company First Squad Guide

Start with Hawks and three complementary Operators when all four slots are open. Cover these jobs with the roster you have: advance toward the objective, reach targets at different ranges, and support teammates who get into trouble.

Spoiler-free

Who should go in your first squad?

Hawks is required on story missions. Skirmishes allow more flexibility, and some missions give you control of additional characters. The standard deployment cap is four Operators, while your company roster at the Den can eventually reach 20.

  • Hawks: Start by reading Hawks' current abilities. Count any job Hawks already covers when filling the other slots. If Hawks falls, the mission fails even with Permadeath disabled.
  • Objective mover: Look for an Operator who can reach the objective from cover and still have an action available. A Scout is one option when positioning matters.
  • Damage at a second range: Pair a close-range option with someone who can contribute from farther away. An Assault or Soldier can fill a damage slot if their equipment matches the map.
  • Support: A Medic adds recovery options when available. Otherwise, check who can carry a healing utility and who can reach teammates to Rally them. If your lineup already covers recovery, use the spare slot for the mission's biggest gap.

Use the objective to break a tie. A timed interaction makes mobility more valuable. A holdout favors Operators who can use Overwatch or a support ability after moving into position. Treat these jobs as a checklist for your available roster.

Plan your opening turn

  1. Read the objective first. Check whether you need to move toward a marker, protect a location, or clear a threat. Take note of any turn counter.
  2. Budget three Action Points (AP) per Operator. Preview the movement cost and check how much the next action will cost before committing. Your whole squad acts in the same turn.
  3. Check the hit preview. If a key attack has poor odds, try a better angle from cover, select another Operator, or use an ability that helps the squad's next action.
  4. End with a fallback. Check each Operator's cover and remaining AP. If you use Overwatch, make it that Operator's last action because it immediately ends their turn.

Example turn · A contested objective

Imagine a mission marker with a covered tile nearby. If the UI shows that tile costs one AP to reach, your mover has two AP left. Check the actual cost of the objective interaction or next ability before you move. If the total is too high, take the covered position now and have a teammate help during the same squad turn. Leave Hawks and your support Operator able to reach the mover if the plan stalls.

If a teammate is Downed, check whether another Operator can safely reach them and Rally. The teammate can act again after being Rallied and still has an Injury to manage. Bring them back to cover and use the Medbay after the mission. See the Permadeath and injury guide for campaign settings that affect the risk.

Before you deploy again

  • Medbay: Review Injuries. Give an Operator time to recover and choose a replacement who covers the same job.
  • Personnel: Check squad Bonds and any Cross Training options that are available. Keep a second Operator ready to cover important jobs when one teammate is unavailable.
  • Armory: Review weapon range, AP costs, and utility items against the next mission's objective.
